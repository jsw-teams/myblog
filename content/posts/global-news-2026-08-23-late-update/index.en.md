## 1. Singapore turns child support into a long-duration package covering ages 0 to 17

Prime Minister Lawrence Wong announced a new SG Child Support Package at the National Day Rally on Aug 23. From Apr 1, 2027, each Singaporean child can receive close to S$70,000 in government support from birth through age 17, with the baseline package no longer varying by birth order and with children in single-parent families covered as well. The policy timeline is being stretched far beyond the birth years, reflecting the view that the financial pressure of raising a child accumulates across nearly two decades.

## 2. Parents can receive up to 12 days of childcare leave each year

Under the new structure, each parent gets eight days of leave with one Singaporean child aged 12 or below, 10 days with two children and 12 days with three or more. The government will reimburse employers for statutory childcare-related leave costs up to an existing weekly cap of S$2,500. That shifts a larger portion of the direct financial burden of family-friendly leave from employers to public finances.

## 3. Government-supported full-day childcare fees are targeted to fall to S$150 a month by 2030

Wong said fees at government-supported preschools would be reduced significantly, with full-day childcare targeted at S$150 per month and full-day infant care at S$300 by 2030. Reductions are due to begin progressively from 2028. Unlike a one-off baby bonus, lower recurring childcare costs can directly affect whether parents return to full-time employment and how much unpaid care must be supplied by extended families.

## 4. The BTO household income ceiling rises from S$14,000 to S$16,000

From Aug 24, the monthly household income ceiling for new BTO flats rises from S$14,000 to S$16,000, while the executive condominium ceiling rises from S$16,000 to S$18,000. The corresponding ceiling for eligible singles aged 35 and above rises to S$8,000. From February 2027, first-timer families with or expecting Singaporean children will also receive one extra ballot chance per child. Housing policy is being connected more explicitly to family policy.

## 5. Singapore studies linking Semakau, Bukom and Sudong into a larger strategic island

The National Day Rally outlined a long-term plan to study reclamation that could connect Pulau Semakau, Pulau Bukom, Pulau Sudong and other southwestern islands for future industrial, energy and strategic needs. The government is also studying an undersea tunnel connecting mainland Singapore to Pulau Tekong. For a land-constrained city-state, the proposal amounts to reserving a future zone for activities that need large footprints and are difficult to place next to dense residential areas.

## 6. AI risk enters Singapore's highest-profile annual policy speech

Wong explicitly discussed risks from increasingly autonomous AI systems and said safeguards would be needed as capabilities become harder to predict. He also announced stronger protections for children and teenagers on social media, including more robust age verification. The government cited local data showing that roughly one in six people aged 10 to 24 show signs of problematic social-media use. Singapore is accelerating AI commercialization while simultaneously building tools to restrict higher-risk uses.

## 7. Singapore loosens restrictions on dialect films and subscription television

In his Mandarin speech, Wong said Singapore could make more room for Chinese dialects while continuing to promote Mandarin. From September, cinemas will no longer face a cap on the number of dialect-film screenings as long as a Mandarin-dubbed version is also available, while restrictions on dialect content on subscription television will also be removed. The shift is modest, but it marks a tangible loosening in a language policy environment that has long favored Mandarin in mass media.

## 8. Immigration and multiracial policy are again framed together

Wong said Singapore would remain open to newcomers while broadly maintaining the country's existing ethnic balance and preserving space for the languages, cultures and traditions of different communities. Aging and low fertility make immigration difficult to remove from population policy, but faster inflows also feed directly into debates over identity, housing and public resources. The government's stated direction is continued openness with tighter attention to pace and social continuity.

## 9. Lai Ching-te uses the Kinmen Aug 23 commemoration to argue for peace through strength

Aug 23 marked the 68th anniversary of the 1958 artillery battle around Kinmen. Taiwan President Lai Ching-te attended commemorations on the island and argued that peace has to be protected by strength. The ceremony came days after Taiwan proposed 2027 defense spending above NT$1 trillion and more than 3% of GDP, linking the historical memory of the 1958 crisis directly to current debates over deterrence, gray-zone pressure and cross-Strait military activity.
