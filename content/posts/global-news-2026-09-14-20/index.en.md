## 1. Taiwan conducts its first combined exercise using multiple attack drones and missiles

President Lai Ching-te said the integration of unmanned systems with conventional firepower was a test of how the armed forces are adapting to modern warfare. The exercise moves drones beyond isolated reconnaissance or attrition missions and places them inside a wider anti-ship, anti-landing and long-range-fire network. It also connects directly to Taiwan’s proposed defense budget of more than NT$1 trillion next year and the U.S. arms package that Washington has delayed.

## 4. Micron Taiwan’s union keeps a strike threat alive over permanent profit sharing

The union representing most Micron workers in Taiwan said on September 15 that it would continue preparing for a strike unless the company created a permanent, transparent and verifiable profit-sharing system. No strike has been called and production has not been affected, but Taiwan is a crucial base for Micron’s DRAM and HBM output. The dispute shows how the AI boom is turning labor relations into a question of how excess supply-chain profits are distributed.

## 9. The United States publicly acknowledges that it has deployed on-orbit space-control weapons

Air Force Secretary Troy Meink said this week that the United States had deployed weapons capable of carrying out space-control missions in orbit, without identifying the systems. The Space Force said those capabilities could include kinetic and non-kinetic methods for offensive or defensive use. The 1967 Outer Space Treaty bans weapons of mass destruction in space but does not comprehensively ban conventional weapons. The admission therefore may not itself violate the treaty, but it is likely to accelerate a U.S.-China-Russia arms race in orbit.
